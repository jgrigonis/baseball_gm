Yes, MLB teams occasionally intentionally walk elite power hitters with no runners on 
base and no outs. While rare, this strategy is employed to avoid a high-probability 
home run or extra-base hit, preferring to take their chances with the following 
batter (the "on-deck" hitter), especially in high-leverage situations. 


Rationale: The goal is to bypass a dangerous hitter (e.g., Shohei Ohtani or 
Barry Bonds) and start with a fresh matchup against a less intimidating batter,
even though it puts a runner on base.

Context: This is more common in late-inning, close-game scenarios, or when an 
elite hitter is facing a pitcher they have historically dominated.

Significance: While generally avoided early in games due to the risk of creating a 
scoring opportunity, it is an accepted, albeit debated, defensive strategy to 
minimize damage.

The MLB season is a marathon, with each team playing 162 regular 
season games over roughly six months, from lateMarch/early April 
to late September/early October, followed by a month-long postseason 
in October/November, starting with Spring Training in February for 
preparation. 

Opening Day is the last thursday of march or first thursday of April


Since 2023, the format is balanced, ensuring every team plays all 29 opponents annually.

Key Components of the 162-Game Schedule:
Intradivision (52 games): 13 games against each of the four divisional 
opponents (4 series of 3-4 games).

Maximum Consecutive Games: The Collective Bargaining Agreement (CBA) 
generally restricts teams from playing more than 20 consecutive days 
without a day off.

All-Star Break: The most significant break occurs in mid-July, 
providing a 4-day rest for most players.

Intraleague (64 games): 6 or 7 games against the other 10 teams in the 
same league (American or National).

Interleague (46 games): 1 series (3 games) against 14 teams in the 
opposite league, plus a 4-game home-and-home series against a 
designated "natural rival" (e.g., Cubs-White Sox, Mets-Yankees). 

A "home-and-home" in MLB refers to a series of games where the two 
teams play at least one game at each team’s respective home stadium. 
It typically involves a short, consecutive series, such as two games 
at Team A's ballpark followed by two games at Team B's ballpark. 
Structure: It breaks up the traditional long home-stand or road trip 
by splitting the series location.
Purpose: Usually done to manage travel, create a quick back-to-back 
series, or to balance home/away games between geographical rivals.
Example: The Yankees play two games in Boston, then immediately travel 
for two games in New York against the Red Sox. 

Travel Constraints: Teams rarely play a night game followed by a 
day game without a day off in between, especially when traveling 
long distances.

Special Requests: Teams often request home games on specific 
dates (e.g., Red Sox on Patriots' Day) or avoid them during city events.

Rotation: Matchup locations (home/away) for interleague games 
alternate annually.

Ending: The regular season finishes with all teams playing 
simultaneously to prevent advantages in the final standings.

Pitcher rotations shift from a five-man, 162-game structure to a tighter 
three- or four-man rotation in the playoffs to maximize top-tier talent. 
Due to increased off-days, starters operate on less rest, often throwing 
fewer than five innings to allow aggressive bullpen usage. Fifth starters 
typically move to a relief role, with managers utilizing "openers" or 
bullpen games. 

Typical rest for an MLB starting pitcher is 4 to 5 days between starts, 
with the 5-man rotation being the standard system (pitching every 5th day). 
While historically 4 days was common, many teams now use 5+ days of rest 
to prioritize arm recovery and health, with average rest days often landing 
around 4.80.

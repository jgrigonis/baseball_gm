To call up a Minor League player not on the 40-man roster, an 
MLB team must first create a spot by removing a current player 
via trade, release, assignment to the 60-day injured list, or 
designating a player for assignment (DFA). Once a spot opens, 
the team adds the player to the 40-man roster and then promotes 
them to the 26-man active roster. 

Key Steps and Considerations:
Creating a 40-Man Spot: If the 40-man roster is full, the team 
must remove someone. The most common method is designating a 
player for assignment, which gives the team 7 days (reduced 
from 10 in 2022) to trade, release, or try to pass that player 
through outright waivers to the minors.

Adding the New Player: The team selects the contract of the 
minor leaguer, adding them to the 40-man roster, making them 
eligible for the 26-man active roster.

Options & Service Time: Once added, the player can be optioned 
back down to the minors, but they will burn through their 3-4 
option years.

Exceptions: If a player is being added to replace a player on 
the 60-day IL, a 40-man spot might not need to be cleared, 
depending on existing roster vacancies. 

This process is strictly governed to ensure that players must
 be on the 40-man roster to appear in a Major League game, 
 preventing teams from simply cycling in outside players 
 without formal, binding roster moves.

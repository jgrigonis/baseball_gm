Center Field (CF) The center fielder is often considered the "captain" of 
the outfield and is typically the best defender. This position requires the 
most speed and range to cover the largest area of the field, as well as 
excellent judgment in tracking fly balls. Center fielders are responsible 
for communicating with and directing their corner outfield teammates.

Right Field (RF) Right fielders traditionally have the strongest and most 
accurate throwing arms among the outfielders because they have the longest 
throws to third base and home plate. Left-handed batters tend to hit the 
ball to right field (opposite field for a righty), and while fewer balls 
may be hit there in general, the throws required are critical to prevent 
runners from advancing.

Left Field (LF) The left fielder generally fields the second-most balls, 
particularly from right-handed pull-hitters, but typically does not need 
as strong an arm as the right fielder because throws to the infield are 
shorter. Players with strong offensive skills who may be weaker defensively 
often play in left field. 

First Baseman (1B) (Number 3):
Role: Covers first base, fields throws from other infielders (especially 
on bunts or slow rollers), needs to stretch out.
Skills: Soft hands to scoop low throws, good agility, can be less athletic 
than other infielders; often played by left-handers.

Second Baseman (2B) (Number 4):
Role: Covers the area between first and second base, shares responsibility 
for second base with the shortstop.
Skills: Quick reflexes, good hand-eye coordination, solid range, good arm 
but generally less than a shortstop.

Third Baseman (3B) (Number 5):
Role: Covers third base area, fields hard-hit balls ("the hot corner") and 
bunts, makes long throws to first.
Skills: Excellent reaction time, strong arm for the long throw across the 
diamond, good fielding instincts.

Shortstop (SS) (Number 6):
Role: Covers the area between second and third base, considered the most 
demanding defensive position due to range and throwing.
Skills: Elite range, quickness, strong arm, agility, leadership; often the 
best overall infielder

Catcher: Crouches directly behind the homeplate, wearing a mask, chest 
protector, and shin guards for protection from fast pitches and foul tips.
Pitch Management: Signals pitches to the pitcher, receives pitches, and is 
responsible for blocking wild pitches or balls in the dirt.
Defensive Leader: Calls the game, directs infielders (especially on cutoffs 
and relays), and is the last line of defense at home plate, tagging runners
trying to score.
Base Stealing: Throws to bases to catch runners stealing, requiring quick 
transfers and strong arms.

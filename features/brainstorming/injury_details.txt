When an MLB player is injured, they are moved from the 26-man 
active roster to the Injured List (IL), freeing up a spot to 
call up a replacement, while usually remaining on the 40-man 
roster. Position players use a 10-day IL, while pitchers/two-way 
players use a 15-day IL, with both allowing for retroactive, or 
backdated, placement up to three days. 

Injured List Mechanics
10/15-Day IL: Players are removed from the 26-man active roster 
but still occupy one of the 40 spots on the 40-man roster.
60-Day IL: Used for long-term injuries. Players on this list are 
removed from both the 26-man and 40-man rosters, allowing the team 
to add a replacement player without needing to clear a spot through 
waivers or trades.
7-Day IL: Specifically for concussions.
Rehabilitation Assignments: Players can leave the team to play in 
the minor leagues to prepare for their return. 

Roster Management
Replacing a Player: To add a player to the 26-man roster, they must 
already be on the 40-man roster. If not, the team must 
"purchase their contract" and add them to the 40-man.
40-Man Limit: If the 40-man roster is full, a player must be removed 
via DFA (Designated for Assignment), trade, or by moving another 
player to the 60-day IL.
Retroactive Dating: IL stints can be backdated up to three days from 
the last game played to maximize efficiency. 

For example, if a pitcher is placed on the 15-day IL, the team can 
call up a player already on their 40-man roster to fill the 26-man 
spot immediately. If that pitcher is later moved to the 60-day IL, 
the team gets an extra 40-man spot for another player

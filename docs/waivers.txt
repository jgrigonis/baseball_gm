Waivers in MLB are a procedural mechanism allowing a team to 
remove a player from their 40-man roster, offering other teams 
a 48-hour window to claim them and assume their contract. Primarily 
used to demote players to the minors (outright waivers) or release 
them, this process promotes roster flexibility and competitive balance. 

Key Aspects of MLB Waivers
Claiming Order: If a player is placed on waivers, all 29 other teams 
have a chance to claim him. Priority is determined by reverse winning 
percentage (the team with the worst record gets first choice).

Outright Waivers: Used when a team wants to send a player to the minors, 
but the player is out of options. If he clears (no one claims him), he 
can be assigned to the minor leagues.

Release Waivers: Requested when a team intends to give a player his 
unconditional release.

Designated for Assignment (DFA): When a player is DFA'd, they are 
immediately removed from the 40-man roster and placed on waivers, 
usually within 7 days, to be either traded, claimed, or outrighted.

Clearing Waivers: If a player is not claimed within 48 hours, 
they "clear waivers." The team may then send them to the minors, 
keep them, or release them.

Player Rights: Players with more than five years of service time
can refuse an outright assignment to the minor leagues and elect 
free agency instead. 

Waivers prevent teams from hoarding talent and provide a mechanism 
for struggling teams to add players, often at the cost of the player's 
remaining salary.

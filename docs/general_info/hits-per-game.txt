The average number of hits in an MLB game has hovered around 8.2 
to 8.4 per team in recent seasons (2022-2025), meaning a typical 
game sees about 16-17 total hits between both teams, though it 
fluctuates slightly yearly, with slightly fewer hits in 2024 (8.20) 
than 2023 (8.40). This reflects the overall offensive environment, 
where hits are generally plentiful, though strikeouts have increased 
in recent years, making the hit-to-strikeout ratio a notable trend.  

Key Figures: 
2025: ~8.26 hits per team (total ~16.5).
2024: ~8.20 hits per team (total ~16.4).
2023: ~8.40 hits per team (total ~16.8).


Most Hits (Any Length): 36. The Philadelphia Phillies set the National League 
and all-time record on August 17, 1894, against the Louisville Colonels.

Most Hits (Extra Innings): 33. The Cleveland Indians set this modern-era 
(since 1900) record in an 18-inning game against the Philadelphia Athletics
on July 10, 1932. Ironically, Cleveland lost the game 18–17.

Most Hits (9-Inning Game): 31. This has been achieved twice in the modern era:
    Milwaukee Brewers vs. Toronto Blue Jays (August 28, 1992).
    New York Giants vs. Cincinnati Reds (June 9, 1901).

Brewers Series Record: In August 2025, the Milwaukee Brewers set a franchise 
record for most hits in a three-game series with 56 hits against the Washington 
Nationals.

Blue Jays Series Record: In 2025, the Toronto Blue Jays broke the MLB 
modern-era record for hits in a three-game series with 63 hits against 
the Colorado Rockies.

Franchise Records: On June 24, 2023, the Los Angeles Angels recorded a 
franchise-record 28 hits in a 25–1 victory over the Colorado Rockies. 

The record for most combined hits by both teams in a single game is 58, occurring 
during the aforementioned 18-inning contest between the Athletics (25 hits) and 
Indians (33 hits) in 1932

Over the entire history of Major League Baseball (since 1876), the average is about 
two no-hitters per year. In the modern era (since 1901), the average has been closer 
to three per season

However, the frequency has varied greatly by era and even recently has fluctuated a lot:
Recent Trend (2020s): There has been a significant increase in recent years, with nine 
no-hitters in the 2021 season (an all-time single-season record), and four in each
of the 2022, 2023, and 2024 seasons.

Atypical Season: The 2025 season was an anomaly, becoming the first year since 2005 to 
have zero regular season no-hitters.

Historical Highs: The modern-era record for most no-hitters in a single season is seven, 
which occurred in 1990, 1991, 2012, and 2015.

Historical Lows: The decade of the 1930s only saw eight no-hitters in 10 years. 
The variability is high, meaning some seasons may have many while others have none, but 
the general expectation based on recent trends is around 3-4 no-hitters annually.

Yes, players on the 40-man roster almost always play somewhere; 
they are not just sitting in limbo. The 40-man roster constitutes 
the entire group of players signed to Major League contracts, 
but only a subset (typically 26 players) are on the active MLB 
roster at one time. 

When not on the 26-man active roster, 40-man players are usually 
active in the minor leagues, rehabbing from injury, or waiting for 
a call-up. Here is where they play: 

1. The Active Minor League System (AAA/AA)
Players on the 40-man roster who are not in the Major Leagues are 
often sent ("optioned") to the team's Triple-A or Double-A affiliates. 

Purpose: They continue to play daily while remaining under Major 
League control, acting as immediate depth in case of injury or poor 
performance by active MLB players.
"Optioned" Status: Players on the 40-man roster have "options," 
allowing them to be sent down and recalled up to three times without 
needing to pass through waivers. 

2. Major League Rehabilitation (Rehab)
If a player on the 40-man roster is injured, they are placed on the 
Major League Injured List (IL). 

Where they play: While recovering, they are assigned to a minor 
league affiliate for a rehab assignment to get back into game shape.
Duration: Position players can rehab for up to 20 days, and pitchers 
for up to 30 days, while still on the 40-man roster and earning their 
major league salary. 

3. The 60-Day Injured List 
If a player is severely injured, they can be transferred to the 60-day 
IL, which removes them from the 40-man roster temporarily to free up a 
spot for another player. They still belong to the organization and will 
play once healthy. 

4. September Call-ups 
Historically, the entire 40-man roster was added to the active roster in 
September. Under current rules, the roster expands from 26 to 28 on 
September 1, but those 28 still must be chosen from the 40-man roster. 

In summary: Being on the 40-man roster means a player is in the major 
league organization’s "reserve list" and is either playing in the big 
leagues, playing in the minors, or rehabbing an injury. 

Benefits of Being on the 40-Man
Higher Pay: Players on the 40-man roster earn a significantly higher 
salary than standard minor leaguers, even when playing in the minors.
Rule 5 Protection: The primary reason for the 40-man roster is to protect 
players from being selected by other teams in the Rule 5 Draft.

Big League Camp: They are automatically invited to Major League Spring Training.

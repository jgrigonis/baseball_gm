Defensive Stats

Shortstop

A typical starting Major League Baseball (MLB) shortstop generally 
records between 200 and 250 putouts in a full, healthy season.

A typical starting Major League Baseball (MLB) shortstop records 
between 350 and 450 assists in a full, healthy season

A typical starting shortstop in Major League Baseball (MLB) generally 
commits between 10 and 20 errors in a full 162-game season

Catcher

A typical number of errors for a starting MLB catcher in a full season 
(roughly 100–130 games) is very low, generally ranging from 2 to 6 errors. 
In modern baseball, top-tier defensive catchers frequently record only 
1–3 errors over the entire season.

A typical number of putouts for a starting Major League Baseball (MLB) 
catcher in a full season (approx. 110–125 games played) is 800 to 1,000+

Putouts are largely dictated by how many strikeouts the starting pitcher 
records and the total number of games or innings the catcher plays behind 
the plate, as they earn a putout on every strikeout and most pop-ups, 
while tag-outs on stolen base attempts are less frequent.

In modern Major League Baseball, a typical starting catcher will record 
approximately 40 to 60 assists. While top-tier defensive catchers (like 
J.T. Realmuto or Salvador Perez) can exceed 60–70 assists due to their 
high volume of innings and reputation for catching runners, the 
league-wide average for starters is generally lower due to reduced 
stolen base attempts compared to historical eras

In Major League Baseball (MLB), a typical number of passed balls for a 
primary starting catcher playing a full season (approx. 100–120 games) 
is between 3 and 7. 

Passed balls are relatively rare because catchers are highly skilled at 
blocking; most balls that get past them are officially ruled as 
"wild pitches," which are blamed on the pitcher rather than the catcher. 

Elite/Gold Glove Level: Top-tier defensive catchers often commit 0 to 3 
passed balls in a full season. For example, in 2025, Cal Raleigh played 
over 1,000 innings without a single passed ball.

Average Starter: A typical starting catcher usually records 4 to 8 passed 
balls per year.

High-Range Performance: Catchers who struggle with blocking or who catch 
pitchers with "difficult" repertoires (like high-velocity sinkers or 
knuckleballs) may reach 10 to 15.

First baseman

In Major League Baseball (MLB), a typical number of assists for a 
starting first baseman playing a full season (approx. 140–160 games) 
generally falls between 80 and 120. League Leaders: The players who 
lead their respective leagues in assists often record between 130 and 
160 assists per season.

High-Range Performance: Exceptionally active or "range-heavy" first 
basemen occasionally exceed 160 assists.

In Major League Baseball (MLB), a typical number of putouts for a 
starting first baseman playing a full 162-game season is between 1,100 
and 1,300. League Leaders: The players who lead their league in putouts 
often record between 1,250 and 1,500

In modern Major League Baseball (MLB), a typical number of errors for a 
starting first baseman playing a full season is between 4 and 9. 

First basemen generally have some of the highest fielding percentages on 
the field (often .994 to .997) because the vast majority of their "chances" 
are catches of throws from other infielders, which are rarely ruled as errors 
on the first baseman

Elite/Gold Glove Level: Top defenders like Christian Walker or Matt Olson 
typically commit only 2 to 5 errors in a full 162-game season.
